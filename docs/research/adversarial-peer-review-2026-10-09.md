# Adversarial paper review — 2026-10-09

## Review status

This is an internal adversarial review of the publication candidate. It is not an independent external peer review. The review treats the strongest contribution as a candidate software contract, not a new calibration theorem.

## Severity-ranked objections

| Severity | Objection and counterexample or source | Affected claim | Decision and smallest correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Major** | **Novelty may be overstated.** Calibration, construct validity, and measurement invariance already ask whether indicators support intended interpretations or comparable meanings. A typed declaration checker may be an implementation of those concerns rather than a new theory. See Cronbach and Meehl (1955), Vandenberg and Lance (2000), and Sjøberg and Bergersen (2023). | C11; contribution paragraph | Narrowed. The manuscript says the statistical premise is established and claims only an explicit operation-aware contract, implementation, and synthetic challenge. No novelty theorem is claimed. Independent field review is still needed to establish contribution value. |
| **Major** | **Exact declaration equality may refuse valid bridges.** A 30-minute risk and 24-hour risk for the same event can be related by a validated survival or hazard model. A different event label can also be a known deterministic transformation of a common latent state. | C11; direct-pooling contract | Narrowed to direct pooling under currently supported declarations. The manuscript now states that the checker is not a complete theory and does not implement a general validated transformation or utility bridge. Test bridge authorization and validation before adding it. |
| **Major** | **Cross-outcome decision comparison can be valid.** A stockout forecast and a service-failure forecast may concern different events yet inform one action under a declared state mapping and common loss matrix. Direct probability pooling would still be wrong. | C11; conclusion | Retained with a clear operation distinction. The manuscript gives this counterexample and limits the current refusal to unsupported direct pooling. Add an executable decision-utility bridge only after an independently specified test. |
| **Moderate** | **Mixtures can be legitimate estimands.** If a lane is selected with a known probability before observing its outcome, the weighted mean can estimate the mixture-event rate. If lane membership is hidden or weights are chosen after results, the same calculation answers an unclear or biased question. | C9; Benchmark A discussion | Retained. The manuscript names the 50:50 synthetic mixture and says it is not one common event. Keep weights and target ID next to every pooled number. |
| **Major** | **Observation-process declarations do not prove observation equivalence.** Sampled logs and complete traces can use the same label text but differ in censoring, missingness, or detection sensitivity. Equal metadata can also omit a relevant collection difference. | C11; semantic-conformance claim | Narrowed. The manuscript says the checker compares declarations and cannot verify pipeline truth. A future independent study must inspect sampling, censoring, adjudication, and label provenance, not just compare field values. |
| **Critical** | **There are too few independent experimental cases for a general performance claim.** Benchmark A is a deterministic, two-lane synthetic construction. Its repeated records are not independent target pairs. | C9; abstract and results | Narrowed to a reproducible counterexample. The manuscript reports no population estimate, field error rate, power claim, or real JIT/AIOps validation. New evidence must sample independently authored target pairs and real observation processes. |
| **Critical** | **Reference outcomes were authored inside the same project as the policy.** The policy may encode distinctions used to write its evaluator labels. The 23-case result is not an unbiased external estimate, even though the labels are stored separately from policy code. | C10; operation-specific result | Narrowed to finite fixture counts. The manuscript reports that the labels were not independently adjudicated and that simpler rules remain competitive on some outcomes. Use blinded, independent case authors and adjudicators before a general value claim. |
| **Critical** | **Synthetic cases cannot establish field relevance.** Synthetic targets omit real label noise, source linking errors, system drift, workload costs, and deployment actions. A perfect fixture check does not show usefulness on production incidents or defect labels. | C9, C10; title and framing | Retained as a limitation. The title preserves the existing flagship framing, while the abstract and conclusion explicitly say the results do not establish field performance or validate real-world JIT/AIOps comparisons. |
| **Major** | **Exact replay can be mistaken for replication.** Byte-identical model runs on one case set show repeatability of that frozen execution, not independent evidence across tasks, prompts, versions, machines, or labels. | Companion claims C2–C8; replication report | Retained as a companion result only. The report calls this exact replay and explicitly denies independent replication. It is not used to support the flagship empirical claim. |
| **Major** | **A finite negative pooling result has a narrow logical reach.** Showing that equal calibration summaries can arise for distinct targets disproves calibration sufficiency. It does not show that all cross-family pooling is invalid or that every differing outcome is incommensurable. | C9, C11; conclusion | Narrowed. The manuscript allows explicit mixture estimands and common-utility decision comparisons. It makes no universal prohibition claim and does not infer the status of any real dataset pair. |

## Reviewer assessment

The manuscript now states the defensible result: calibration for one target does not establish that another forecast concerns the same target. The implementation contributes a checkable declaration boundary, but the paper has not shown that the operation-specific rule generalizes or outperforms a simpler rule on independently judged cases. The 23-case false-permission count is a fixture result, not evidence of population performance.

The highest-value next evidence is a blinded, independently authored target-pair study with both false-permission and false-refusal cases, independent pipeline review, and preregistered semantic-bridge positives. A field study should follow only after the target and observation-process mapping is independently defensible.
